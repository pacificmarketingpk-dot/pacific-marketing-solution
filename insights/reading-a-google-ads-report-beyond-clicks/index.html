Clicks are not results: reading a Google Ads report from click to conversion

A $1,720 campaign for Naples Dent Away, followed step by step from impressions to cost per conversion.

2 min read · Based on: Naples Dent Away

At a glance

25Kimpressions
595clicks
9conversions
$1,720total spend

Naples Dent Away, a dent repair service in Naples, Florida, ran a Google Ads campaign to raise its online visibility. Here is how we read the report.

Step 1: did people see and click the ads?

The ads were shown 25,000 times and got 595 clicks, a click-through rate of about 2.4% (calculated). The click-through rate was strong, so the ads and keywords were reaching the right people.

Step 2: what did a click cost?

With $1,720 spent across 595 clicks, the average cost per click was about $2.89.

Step 3: how many clicks became enquiries?

Nine conversions from 595 clicks is a conversion rate of about 1.5% (calculated), or roughly $191 per conversion (calculated). This is the number that decides whether a campaign pays for itself.

What the report points to

Strong clicks but few conversions means the weak spot is after the click: the landing page, the offer, or how easily a visitor can get in touch. That is why the report named conversion rate optimisation as the clear next area.

Illustration only, not a result: if the same 595 clicks converted at 3% instead of 1.5%, the campaign would produce about 18 conversions and the cost per conversion would fall to about $96. Improving the page can matter more than buying more clicks.

What we would do next

  • Check the landing page on a phone: speed, a call button and a clear next step.
  • Track calls and form submissions as conversions.
  • Send each ad group to a page that matches the search.
  • Review search terms and add negative keywords.

Figures come from the Google Ads dashboard screenshots supplied with the project. Calculated figures are marked.
